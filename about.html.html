About

Hi, I'm Pax.

I grew up moving around — New York, Bangkok, London, York. Arriving somewhere new means figuring out unfamiliar systems from scratch: how to pay, how to navigate, which apps actually work here. You stop assuming anything will make sense. That habit of approaching things without preconceptions turned out to be a useful foundation for research.

Back in Bangkok, my parents and grandparents would regularly hand me their phones with some version of the same question: why isn't this working, or why can't I do this thing. I couldn't always fix it. But I started to notice that their frustration was usually reasonable. The product had made assumptions about who was using it, and they just didn't fit.

I've spent two summers at Arise by INFINITAS, working on PaoTang — a government-linked financial app used by tens of millions of people in Thailand. In 2025, I studied QR payment flows across five Thai banking apps, starting from my own observations about friction in time-sensitive payment situations. In 2026, I moved into research: moderating usability sessions for a proposed homepage redesign, building synthesis artefacts, and eventually presenting findings independently to a room of product owners, UX designers, and business analysts.

That's the kind of work I want to keep doing. Finding where the gap is between what a product assumes and what people actually need — and being involved in deciding what to do about it. That sits across research, product, and strategy, and I'm interested in all three.
